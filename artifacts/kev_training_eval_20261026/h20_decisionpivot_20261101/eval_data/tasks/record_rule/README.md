# record_rule

Generated record batches, second version: which integrity rule does the batch violate? (layer F, generated, programmatic truth; version v2, set gen_v4 of 2026-10-08)

- gen_v4 (2026-10-08) changes three things of the v2 set and nothing else in its design: (1) the background of every batch is consistent - outside the planted violation and the one or two deliberate oddities, tax = round(net x tax_rate / 100) and every stored total holds, the second date is not before the first, and a record that names an earlier record (a recheck, a credit, a reopened ticket ...) is dated after it; (2) a state with a status column in a reference list says what a status counts for; (3) the share of `none` is asserted at every level. See "Background data" below.
- Decision: which of the 6 to 9 listed integrity rules a batch of records breaks - in one place, by a near miss - or none.
- Source: generator `build/gen_record_rule.py` (standard library only, `random.Random(20261007)` and generators derived from it; two runs give identical files); no third-party text.
- Version: this directory holds **v2**, the harder second version. The first version (6 / 10 / 14 records, 4 to 7 rules of six kinds that read one field or one record; strong models reach 0.98 to 1.00 on it) is regenerated byte for byte by setting `DIFFICULTY = "v1"` at the top of the script (or `BENCH_DIFFICULTY=v1`), which rewrites this directory. `BENCH_OUT=<dir>` writes to `<dir>/record_rule/` instead; `BENCH_SEED=<k>` (v2 only, together with `BENCH_OUT`) makes another item set of the same design.
- Licence: generated by the benchmark's script (no third-party text); items included.
- Rows: 600 = 150 batches x 4 items (`group`); options: R1 ... Rk (k = 6..9, option text = the sentence of the rule) and `none`, 10 at most; metric: accuracy (chance 0.1198, the mean of 1 / number of options).
- Rebuild: `python3 build/gen_record_rule.py`

## What is generated

A state is one text: a lead-in with the two general conventions, the field list of the record type, 6 to 9 numbered integrity rules, the reference lists that the rules use, and a batch of 10, 16 or 24 records. The question is "Which rule does the batch violate?"; the options are the keys R1 ... Rk, each with the sentence of its rule as option text, and `none`; their order is drawn per item (`meta.order`). A batch breaks at most one rule, in one place. Gold = the key of the broken rule, or `none`.

Eight invented record types (order lines, shift entries, sensor readings, library loans, support tickets, parcels, course enrolments, invoice lines) with 21 fields each. A state shows 11 to 17 of them: the key, the fields of the listed rules and the fields of up to two kinds that the schema does NOT list (room for oddities that no rule covers). The field order is drawn per batch. Two layouts of the records (JSON lines; a table with " | " between the cells) and two of the reference lists (on one line; one entry per line), four phrasings of every rule kind (sixteen kinds of sentence: thirteen rule kinds, the strict date order, product and sum), four lead-ins, four wordings of the general conventions, three of every header. Thresholds, allowed lists, list sizes, statuses and strictness are drawn per batch; identifiers and names are code-like patterns or come from a syllable generator.

Thirteen rule kinds: the six of the first version and seven that need a computation, a second record or a second list. A schema lists 6, 7, 8 or 9 rules of different kinds, 3 to 5 of them of the seven new kinds (the share of the new kinds is 7 / 13 for every number of rules, and every kind is listed in the same number of batches).

| kind (`meta.deviation`) | the rule | what has to be worked out | the planted near miss | valid look-alike in every batch that lists the rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `unique` | a key field has no duplicate in the batch | compare every key with every other key | the key of another record that stands at least two records away | 1 / 2 / 3 pairs of near-identical keys at level 1 / 2 / 3 (one digit changed or two neighbouring digits exchanged) |
| `reference` | a field is a key of a reference list (a status column of that list is not asked about) | look every value up in the list | a key with one digit changed or two digits exchanged that the list does not hold | a key in use whose near-identical neighbour is in the list too; half of these lists show a status, and then entries of any status may be in use |
| `range` | a whole number lies between two limits, both included | compare with both limits | one step above the upper or below the lower limit | a value exactly on a limit |
| `conditional` | when a yes/no field says "yes", a second field must be filled in | read the trigger, then the second field | the second field emptied on a record that needs it, or the trigger set on a record whose second field is empty | records without the trigger whose second field is empty |
| `date_order` | one date is not after another (same day allowed) or strictly before it (same day not allowed); the sentence says which | compare two dates | the later date one day (or two to three days) early; under a strict rule also the same day | the same day under a rule that allows it, exactly one day apart under a strict rule; from level 2 a pair across a month end, where the later date has the smaller day number |
| `allowed` | a field takes one of three or four listed words | compare with the list | another word of the same vocabulary, where possible one that the sibling field uses in the same batch | an allowed word that occurs once only; in most batches a sibling field without a rule that uses the other words |
| `composite` | the PAIR of two fields occurs once; each of the two fields repeats | compare two fields across pairs of records | a record that shared one of the two fields with another record now shares both | records that share the first field only and records that share the second field only, with near-identical values among them |
| `ref_active` | a field is a key of a reference list AND the list marks that key with the status named in the rule | find the key in the list, then read its status | a key that the list holds with another status; often the near-identical neighbour of the key the record had | the active neighbour of an inactive key is in use; inactive entries that no record names |
| `dep_range` | the limits of a number depend on a second field (two branches whose spans overlap in part) | read the branch, take its limits, compare | a value one step outside its branch (or further) that the other branch allows, or the branch field changed on a record whose value only the old branch allows | values on a limit of their branch, and in both branches values that the other branch forbids |
| `xor` | exactly one of two fields is filled in | read both fields | both filled in, or both empty | records that fill only the first field and records that fill only the second |
| `derived` | a stored total equals the product (three record types) or the sum (five record types) of two other fields | one multiplication or addition per record | two neighbouring digits of the total exchanged, or the total off by 1, 2 or 10 | none of its own: every record needs the computation |
| `sequence` | a number rises by exactly one from each record to the next; the first number is free | compare neighbours down the batch | one number skipped or repeated at one step (the numbers on the shorter side of the step move by one, so there is one broken step) | the count never starts at 1; in about a third of the batches it passes from 9 to 10 or from 99 to 100 |
| `self_ref` | a field that, when filled in, names the key of a record that stands EARLIER in the batch | find the named key among the records, compare the two places | the key of a record further down, or the named key with one digit changed (a key that no record has) | the record just before is named; where the batch has room for it, an earlier key is named although a near-identical key follows later |

The first six kinds are those of the first version; their sentences are new, and their violations are now near misses as well.

## Conventions written into every state

- General (four wordings): at most one rule is broken, and only in one place; a rule asks for exactly what its sentence says; whatever no listed rule covers is not a violation.
- Every rule sentence carries its own convention: limits included; the same day allowed or not; which field may repeat; the first number of a running count is free; a named record must stand earlier in the batch; the status that a list entry must have.
- The header of the records says that they stand in batch order and how an empty field looks: in the JSON layout a field that is missing or null, in the table a cell that holds only "-". "Filled in" is the opposite of empty.
- A reference list with statuses is written `key: status`. A paragraph after the lists says what a status counts for (gen_v4; four wordings, one per batch, in the 420 states that show a status column): a plain reference rule - one that only asks for a key of the list - accepts every key the list holds, whatever its status, and only a rule that names a status (`ref_active`) requires it. In 144 items a record names a key of unusual status under a plain reference rule (`list_status` among the oddities); a reader who asked a status of that rule would find a violation that is not there (or a second one) in 128 of them.
- Fields and list names stand in backticks inside the rules, text values in double quotes, numbers and dates (YYYY-MM-DD) bare.

## Background data (gen_v4)

Everything in a batch that no listed rule asks about still behaves like data. Outside the planted violation and the one or two deliberate oddities, every record keeps the following relations (the script checks all of them on all 10000 records of the 600 items: `stray_breaks2`, 0 breaks outside the places named below). `meta.background` lists the relations that hold in a batch.

| relation | where | how it is kept |
|---|---|---|
| a stored total is the product or the sum of its operands (`derived`, listed or shown as an extra kind) | every record type that shows the three fields | drawn that way; the planted `derived` violation changes the total and nothing else |
| tax = round(net x tax_rate / 100), never a tie between two roundings; gross = net + tax | invoice lines that show `tax_rate`, `net` and `tax` | the tax follows the rate of its own record; the planted `range` violation on `tax_rate` moves the tax and the gross with the rate, so that the record breaks the range and nothing else. Without a `tax_rate` field the batch draws its taxes from two usual rates (7, 10, 19, 21 or 25 per cent): the tax is at most a quarter of the net |
| the second date is not before the first (an upload on or after its reading, a delivery after the dispatch ...) | every record type that shows the date pair | gaps of 0 or more days (1 or more under a strict rule) |
| a record that names an earlier record (`recheck_of`, `credit_for`, `replaces`, `corrects`, `renewal_of`, `reopens`, `forwarded_from`, `transfer_of`) is dated after it: a recheck after the reading, a credit after the invoice line | every record type that shows the reference field and the date pair | the references are drawn first and the dates follow record by record: the first date lies at least one day after the first date of the named record (after the closing date for tickets, after the delivery date for parcels); the planted `date_order` violation moves the date that cannot put a naming record before it, and the `self_ref` violation 'a later record' picks, where the batch allows it, a record whose date is in order with the later one it names |

The places where a relation may break are the deliberate oddities of the batch (`meta.oddities`, up to two; 125 of 150 batches have one or two) and the planted record, and each only for the relation of its own kind: a wrong total (`derived`), a reversed date pair (`date_order`), a reference to a later record (`self_ref`). Every other oddity (a repeated key or pair, a skipped number, an empty required field, both or neither of two fields, a key of unusual status) leaves the relations above intact. No field of a record type suggests a relation that the batch does not keep: a loan period or the payment days of an invoice would have to match the date pair, so the limited fields of those two record types are a `deposit` and a `handling_fee`, and the shipping modes of order lines contain no `pickup` next to the pickup / delivery fields.

The number of rules (6 to 9) and the mix of kinds are the same at every level; the level is the size of the batch (and of the lists).

**What makes level 3 hard.** 24 records with up to 17 fields and 6 to 9 rules: every rule has to be cleared on every record, and 3 to 5 of the rules cannot be cleared from one cell. One rule check of a new kind combines at least three facts from different places: for `ref_active` the status named in the rule, the key in the record and the entry of that key among 11 to 14 entries with near-identical neighbours; for `dep_range` the two branches of the rule, the branch field of the record and its value; for `self_ref` the named key, the place of the record that carries this key and the place of the record that names it; for `derived` two operands, one multiplication or addition and the stored total, 24 times; for `composite` two fields of two records among 276 pairs of records; for `sequence` 23 steps. The single violation is one step from valid and may sit anywhere in the batch, valid records sit on the same limits, and most batches also hold something odd that no listed rule covers. Answering `none` needs all of these checks to come out clean.

## Groups and classes

150 batches (50 per level), each used for 4 items (`group`, `meta.batch`). In 72 batches (24 per level, 18 per number of rules) one item is the untouched batch (`none`) and three items carry one planted violation each, of three different rules; in the other 78 batches four different rules are violated. The states of a group have the same number of lines and are identical apart from the planted record (491 of 528 violating items differ from their batch in one line; a `sequence` violation moves the numbers on the shorter side of the broken step, 1 to 12 lines). A violating state is between 15 characters shorter and 7 longer than its batch (median +0, mean -0.22). `none` is 72 of 600 items = 0.120; the designed share is about 1 / (mean number of options) = 0.118 (mean number of options 8.49, chance 0.120). ## Checks made by the script

- `truth2(spec)` evaluates every listed rule on the structured records and returns the places where it is broken: a violating item breaks exactly the gold rule in exactly one place, a `none` item breaks nothing.
- Every batch, and every item made from it, holds a valid look-alike of every listed rule (last column of the table of kinds); a batch or a planted violation without them is drawn again.
- The records and the reference lists read back from the rendered text equal the specification (`meta.spec`); the status paragraph stands in exactly the states that show a status column.
- Consistent background (gen_v4): `stray_breaks2` finds no broken relation (total, tax, date pair, dated reference) outside the planted record and the oddity records, in any of the 600 items; a base batch or a planted violation that breaks one is drawn again.
- Share of `none`: 0.120 / 0.120 / 0.120 at levels 1 / 2 / 3, asserted within 0.005 of 1 / (mean number of options) at every level and overall.
- The four states of a group have the same number of lines and differ from their batch in the planted record only; their options are the same and their gold keys differ.
- Counts: levels, classes and kinds per level, hosts of the `none` items per number of rules, positions of the gold option and of the violated rule, the same chance for every kind to be the broken one among the listed ones; no duplicate state; every state below 14,000 characters (2535 to 10507, median 4751).
- Vocabulary (the `none` items against the violating items): 564 words occur in 20 or more states; none is more than twice as frequent in one class as in the other (largest ratio 1.64). The batches that give the `none` items are chosen for this (3 per cell of level, number of rules and layout, exchanged inside the cells until the words are spread evenly). Numbers are not part of the test: one number stands in a handful of batches, and a batch brings its four items along.
- `python3 validate.py record_rule`: OK. `python3 audit.py record_rule`:

```

```

`audit_v3.py record_rule` (needs numpy) and the independent solver of the benchmark (`solve_all.py`, a second implementation that reads the rendered text alone) are run outside this script.

### level 2: 16 records, 8 rules, table layout (record_rule:00101, none; oddities that are not violations: `composite` in record 6; `conditional` in record 1)

```

Each record has these fields: line_id (text), quantity (whole number), pickup_point (text), discount_pct (whole number), sku (text), gift_note (text), tier (text), order_no (text), unit_price (whole number), replaces (text), ship_mode (text), line_total (whole number), delivery_zone (text), line_no (whole number), shipped_on (date, YYYY-MM-DD), placed_on (date, YYYY-MM-DD), gift (text).
```
